Exhibit D · 21:00 · Crime or chance?

Annualised night − day gap, with its 95% interval

Every interval excludes zero. SPY's comes closest: its lower bound is {{ spy.with.lo }}% a year. Now remove the dividends.

Dividends land at night: on the day a stock goes ex-dividend, its price drops at the open, and the dividend is counted in the overnight return. Without them, SPY ({{ spy.without.est }}%) and equal-weighted stocks ({{ ew.without.est }}%) can no longer be told apart from zero. Part of the loot was a mechanical effect, not a mystery. But not all of it: heavily traded stocks and ETFs keep a clear gap.

Police report · Facts established

  1. The crime is real and robust for large, heavily traded stocks and for ETFs.
  2. For SPY and equal-weighted stocks it is real but fragile: dividends explain {{ f.dividend_share.spy }}% of SPY's gap and {{ f.dividend_share.stocks_ew }}% of the equal-weighted stock gap.
  3. Victim profile: the most traded fifth of stocks shows the largest gap, {{ f.q5.est }}% a year [{{ f.q5.lo }}; {{ f.q5.hi }}]. These stocks tend to draw the most news and attention: a lead for the suspects.
